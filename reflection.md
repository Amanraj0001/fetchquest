~What I got wrong at the start
I initially tried to force my existing learning planner, Coursepath, into the assignment before realizing it didn't really fit the requirement for solving a local problem with personally collected data. I pivoted to FetchQuest because I saw a real, everyday issue: classmates needing quick items from the Basti marketplace but not having the time to go themselves. That gave the project a much clearer, real-world focus.
However, I completely misjudged the timing report. I assumed having 20 trip entries meant I had 20 solid data points. But once I filtered the trips by route, transport mode, and what was actually bought, only one category had enough data (just three observations) to even make a decent estimate. 

~What I would cut
If I could do it over, I’d strip the demo down to the bare essentials: posting a request, accepting it, confirming the delivery, and walking through the timing data. I wasted effort on features like ratings, profile pictures, and heavy admin controls. None of that really matters if I can't prove the core concept—that one classmate can easily get help from another. I should have spent that time taking clearer field notes and actually testing the main user flow with real students.

~Where AI helped
AI was a huge help in brainstorming how my ideas matched the rubric, building out the backend, hooking it up to the frontend, writing tests,It also did the heavy lifting of formatting my raw trip logs, crunching the travel durations, and updating the report when I had to fix my own bad entries. It definitely sped up the coding process.

~Where AI wasted time
Because it’s so easy to generate code, AI led me down a rabbit hole of building a massive backend with auth, chat, ratings, and moderation before I had even nailed down the fieldwork output. I ended up with way too much implementation and review overhead for an assignment that just asked for one small, useful result.
I also had to prompt the AI multiple times just to get the timing report to clearly connect to FetchQuest's actual use case. Next time, I need to figure out exactly what decision the data needs to support *before* writing any code, and then only use AI to build the absolute minimum required to demonstrate it. I can't even calculate how much time I would have saved doing it that way.
